This page is a curated index of the sources behind the safe-C++ conversation. The companion essay is [C++ Safety State of the Union: May 2026](/posts/cpp-safety-state-of-the-union-may-2026/). It predates the ballot, so read the notes below first.

Editorial bias: papers, first-hand industry reports and named authors. No aggregators.

## What changed since May

As of 2026-11-06:

- The ISO ballot on the C++26 draft closed on 22 October and was reported disapproved. Read [Pusz's account](https://train-it.eu/blog/cpp26-contracts-the-ballot-result) (25 October) and [Fertig's](https://andreasfertig.com/blog/2026/11/cpp26-dis-failed-how-does-cpp-survive/) (3 November), who says he is one of Germany's delegates and that the national-body comments point at contracts. They list different ways forward and I do not reconcile them. No official statement exists that I could find. Our posts: <PostLink slug="c26-dis-ballot-result">the ballot</PostLink> and <PostLink slug="contracts-dispute">the dispute</PostLink>.
- [P4433R0](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2026/p4433r0.pdf) (Doumler, Berne and 14 co-authors, 23 October) asks the Búzios meeting to poll balloting again with contracts unchanged. [P4238R1](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2026/p4238r1.pdf) is an "inform" paper on how the contracts decisions were made. P4433R0 disputes some of its claims.
- Stroustrup's profile papers of 22 October: [P3704R1](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2026/p3704r1.pdf), [P4222R3](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2026/p4222r3.pdf) and [P4418R0](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2026/p4418r0.pdf). [P4415R0](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2026/p4415r0.pdf) reports two experimental implementations, covered in <PostLink slug="profiles-p4415-implementation-status">our post</PostLink>.
- The [Chrome Security Q3 2026 update](https://www.chromium.org/Home/chromium-security/quarterly-updates/) reports full MiraclePtr enforcement on all processes and platforms. It also reports a DCHECK to CHECK conversion since Chrome 152, at up to 5000 conversions per milestone until at least Chrome 159.
- CRA reporting obligations apply from 11 September 2026, and ENISA [launched its reporting platform](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/news/the-cra-single-reporting-platform-is-launched) that day.
- Meeting C++ 2026 runs 26 to 28 November. Its first program announcement included Guy Davidson's "Profiles are coming", per [meetingcpp.com](https://meetingcpp.com/meetingcpp/news/items/Announcing-Meeting-Cpp-2026-.html) as quoted by a search result; I did not open the schedule. A session titled "Contracts and hardened libraries" is listed there; I could not confirm its speaker.

## The committee's papers

- [P2900R14 Contracts](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2025/p2900r14.pdf) (Berne, Doumler, Krzemieński et al.): in the C++26 draft. Four evaluation semantics, a replaceable violation handler, per-translation-unit granularity. The Croydon vote to finalize the draft was 114 for, 12 against and 3 abstaining, per [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/03/31/cplusplus26_approved), which ties the dissent to contracts.
- [P3471R4 Standard library hardening](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2025/p3471r4.html) (Varlamov, Dionne): the first standard-library use of contracts. It cites Google's figures, as low as 0.3% cost and over 1000 bugs found.
- [P3878R1](https://wg21.link/p3878r1): the library hardening fix applied in Kona. A library is not hardened if it evaluates hardened preconditions with the `observe` semantic. [P4433R0](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2026/p4433r0.pdf) note 6 explains how it relates to [P4334R0](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2026/p4334r0.pdf).
- [P2996 Reflection](https://wg21.link/p2996) (Childers, Sutton, Revzin, Yang et al.): in the C++26 draft; the link resolves to the latest revision, R13.
- [P3589R2 Profiles: the framework](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2025/p3589r2.pdf) (Dos Reis), [P3984R0 A type-safety profile](https://www.stroustrup.com/type-safety-profile.pdf) (Stroustrup, draft of 28 January 2026) and the three October papers above: the profiles line of work for C++29.
- [P3970R0 Profiles DG statement](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2026/p3970r0.pdf) (Vandevoorde, Garland, McKenney, Orr, Stroustrup, Wong; 16 January 2026): asks for the framework to be implemented so that specific profiles can be tried.
- [P5000R1 Direction for ISO C++29](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2026/p5000r1.pdf) (Directions Group, 6 May 2026).
- [P4158R0 Subsetting and restricting C++ for memory safety](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2026/p4158r0.pdf) (Oliver Hunt): slides reporting about 4 million lines of WebKit code under a subset.
- [P3543R0 Response to P3081](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2024/p3543r0.pdf) (Gill, Jabot, Lakos, Berne, Doumler): the Bloomberg-led counter-paper to the original profiles proposal.
- [P3294 Code injection with token sequences](https://wg21.link/p3294) (Revzin, Alexandrescu, Vandevoorde): latest revision R2 from 2024. The earlier text said it was re-revised in the May 2026 mailing; I found no such revision.

## The industry reports

- [Chromium memory safety page](https://www.chromium.org/Home/chromium-security/memory-safety/): around 70% of serious security bugs are memory safety problems, based on an analysis of 912 high or critical bugs (read 2026-11-06).
- [Chrome Security quarterly updates](https://www.chromium.org/Home/chromium-security/quarterly-updates/): the Q3 2026 entry is the source for the MiraclePtr and DCHECK to CHECK items above.
- [Android memory safety docs](https://source.android.com/docs/security/test/memory-safety): memory safety bugs account for over 60% of high severity vulnerabilities in the Android codebase, and native code is over 70% of the platform code (read 2026-11-06).
- ["Practical Security in Production: Hardening the C++ Standard Library at Massive Scale"](https://research.google/pubs/practical-security-in-production-hardening-c-standard-library-at-massive-scale/) (Dionne, Shavrick, Varlamov; ACM Queue 23(5), 2025): the hardened libc++ rollout at Google. I read the abstract, not the article. The earlier version linked an ACM Queue URL that returns 403 to curl and described it wrongly.
- [Google "Retrofits" Spatial Memory Safety Onto C++](https://thenewstack.io/google-retrofits-spatial-memory-safety-onto-c/) (The New Stack, December 2024): quotes Google's report of an average 0.30% performance impact for hardened libc++, and Chandler Carruth's reaction. The earlier text also credited it with a MiraclePtr 57% figure, Project Naptime and a V8 sandbox; I did not find those in the article and removed them.

## The regulatory references

- [CISA: The Case for Memory Safe Roadmaps](https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/case-memory-safe-roadmaps): the earlier link to the news item returned 404, so this points at the resource page. The [CISA, NSA and FBI joint PDF](https://media.defense.gov/2023/Dec/06/2003352724/-1/-1/0/THE-CASE-FOR-MEMORY-SAFE-ROADMAPS-TLP-CLEAR.PDF) returned 403 to curl. The earlier text gave a January 2026 deadline for manufacturer roadmaps; I could not confirm that date from a page I could read.
- [CISA Secure by Design: product security bad practices](https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/product-security-bad-practices): says that not having a published memory safety roadmap for existing products in memory-unsafe languages is dangerous.
- [EU Cyber Resilience Act](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cyber-resilience-act): in force since 10 December 2024, with reporting obligations from 11 September 2026 and the main obligations from 11 December 2027.
- ["C++ creator calls for action to address 'serious attacks'"](https://www.theregister.com/software/2025/03/02/c-creator-calls-for-action-to-address-serious-attacks/1401456) (The Register, March 2025) and ["C++ founder champions profiles for memory safety"](https://www.infoworld.com/article/3839386/c-plus-plus-founder-champions-profiles-for-memory-safety.html) (InfoWorld, March 2025): the public reports of Stroustrup's warning.
