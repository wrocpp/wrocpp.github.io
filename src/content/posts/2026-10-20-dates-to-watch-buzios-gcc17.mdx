Three dates fall within a month of each other, and two of them land on the same day. All of the dates below were read on their primary pages on 6 October 2026, and none of them is a forecast: each is published as a fixed date.

## 23 October: the pre-Búzios mailing closes

The WG21 home page carries a news item dated 22 September: the deadline for the 2026-10 pre-Búzios mailing is Friday 2026-10-23 15:00 UTC.

If you are writing a paper for the November meeting, that is the cut-off for being in the mailing the committee reads beforehand. The page does not say what a late paper loses, and I did not read the committee's submission rules, so check those before relying on a day or two of slack.

## 16 to 21 November: Búzios

The ISO C++ meetings list gives 2026-11-16 to 21 in Búzios, Rio De Janeiro, Brazil. It does not name a host, and it does not use the word C++29. That comes from Herb Sutter's report on the June meeting in Brno, which calls Brno the first meeting of C++29 and says the next full meeting is Búzios, "where we'll continue adding features to C++29". Calling Búzios the second C++29 meeting is my reading of those two pages, since nothing sits between them.

Brno adopted into the C++29 draft, among other things, contracts for virtual functions (P3097R3), designated initializers for base classes (P2287R6) and `.lookup(key)` for associative containers (P3091). Still in progress per the same report: a line-by-line telecon review of P3100 on undefined behaviour, and a profiles specification from the safety subgroup. The Búzios agenda itself I did not find on any page, so what gets voted in is not known.

The meeting after that is 2027-03-15 to 20 in Istanbul, which is the next date a paper missing Búzios could reach the room.

## 16 November: GCC 17 stage 3

The GCC development page lists GCC 17 stage 3 starting 2026-11-16 and stage 4 starting 2027-01-18. Stage 1 began on 2026-04-23. Its rule for stage 3 is that bug fixes and new ports are allowed and that "New functionality may not be introduced".

For a contributor with a C++ front-end or library patch, the practical reading is that it has to be committed before the 16th. For everyone else, stage 3 is when fixes to trunk stop competing with new features, so a bug report against a trunk build is most useful then. Under that rule, a patch for a feature that is only voted into C++29 in Búzios could not land in GCC 17 after the vote, because the vote and stage 3 begin together. A patch committed before stage 3 is a different case, and I did not check which C++29 papers GCC already implements.

The GCC 17 changes page, last modified 3 October and marked a work in progress, lists no C++ front-end items yet. Under libstdc++ it lists improved experimental C++26 support: `std::format` in constexpr functions and `std::filesystem` support for Windows symlinks.

Meeting C++ in Berlin follows on 26 to 28 November and is [covered separately](/posts/meetingcpp-2026-pusz/).
