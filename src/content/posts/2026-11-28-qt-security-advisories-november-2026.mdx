Between 2 and 16 November 2026 the Qt Company published twelve security advisories on its blog. Two are rated high: a memory corruption in the FFmpeg backend of Qt Multimedia, and a memory exhaustion in an XML reader. I read all twelve pages on 17 November. On that day the download directories listed 6.11.3 as the newest 6.11 release. They listed only 6.12.0 for 6.12, and nothing newer than 6.8.5 for 6.8.

## In short

- Twelve advisories, 2 to 16 November. Two high, two medium and eight low.
- Eleven are scored with CVSS 4.0. The FFmpeg one is scored with CVSS 3.1 (8.8), copied from upstream. The two versions are not comparable, so do not rank the 8.8 against the 8.2.
- Six advisories name Qt 6.12.1 as fixed, and I found no 6.12.1 in the 6.12 directory. Qt 6.12.1 was released on 24 November, after these pages were read (see the end). Eleven name 6.8.9 or 6.8.10, and the 6.8 archive lists no release after 6.8.5.
- Nine pages name Qt 6.11.3 as fixed. It is downloadable, but dated 26 October, and I did not check whether it contains the November fixes.
- Nine pages give a mitigation or advice. Three say none is available.

The pages disagree with themselves in places. The 16 November page lists 6.8.10 as both affected and fixed. The 9 November page does the same with 6.8.10 and 6.12.1. The WebSocket handshake page ends its affected range at 6.12.0. It names no 6.11 fix, yet it links a 6.11 patch archive. Read the patch links for your branch before relying on a version number.

## Where the fixes are

I read the download listings on 17 November.

| directory | newest release listed |
|---|---|
| official releases, Qt 6.12 | 6.12.0, 30 Sep 2026 |
| official releases, Qt 6.11 | 6.11.3, 26 Oct 2026 |
| archive, Qt 6.8 | 6.8.5, 16 Oct 2026 |

The official releases directory has no 6.8 folder (the request returned 404), so for 6.8 I used the archive.

Take the FFmpeg advisory first. It names 6.11.2, dated 18 August in the same listing, so a 6.11 user can already download a release that fixes it. A 6.8 user cannot, because 6.8.9 is not in the archive.

For the other eleven, the picture is less clear. Nine of them name 6.11.3 as fixed, and that release exists. But 6.11.3 is dated 26 October, before every November advisory. I did not unpack it. The 6.11 directory also holds separate patch files for these advisories, for example one for CVE-2026-107479 dated 3 November. That suggests the fixes may not be in the 6.11.3 sources, but I have not confirmed it.

Two advisories, on WebSocket handshakes and HTTP responses, name only 6.8.10 and 6.12.1. Neither is downloadable on 17 November, so those two are patches only.

## What to do now

1. Find the Qt version each shipped binary links, including build images and bundled copies.
2. If you play untrusted media through Qt Multimedia, update to 6.11.2 or later, or rebuild FFmpeg 7.1.5. It is the only page that mentions possible code execution.
3. If you parse XML from outside your process with the Qt 5 compatibility classes, apply the limits from the XML rows. Four of the twelve concern that reader or the DOM parser.
4. For a branch with no fixed release, apply the diffs linked from the advisory. The 6.8 diffs sit on the archive server.
5. Before trusting "6.11.3 or later", diff your 6.11.3 sources against the linked patch.

## After 16 November

Update on 27 November: two things changed after I read the pages above.

- [Qt 6.12.1](https://www.qt.io/blog/qt-6.12.1-released) was released on 24 November. The release post calls it the first patch release of Qt 6.12 LTS. I did not check which of the twelve fixes it contains.
- On 26 November the Qt Company published [CVE-2026-111497](https://www.qt.io/blog/security-advisory-cve-2026-111497-qt-webengine), a buffer overflow in the Brotli decoder bundled with Qt WebEngine. It is rated critical, CVSS 4.0 score 9.2. It affects Qt 6.8.0 to 6.8.9 and 6.9.0 to 6.11.3. The fix is Qt 6.8.10 or Qt WebEngine 6.140.0, and the page advises loading content from trusted sources only until then.

The Qt blog also lists Chromium advisories for WebEngine on 25 and 26 November. I did not read them for this post.

## What this post does not cover

- I did not reproduce any of the twelve issues, and I do not rate them beyond the scores on the pages.
- I read the listing of the Qt blog and each advisory page. Advisories published after 16 November are not in the table.
- I did not unpack any release, and I did not check commercial channels or the Qt Company's own release announcements.
- I did not check the CVE records at MITRE or NVD. Scores and vectors are as the Qt pages give them.
- Third-party packages such as Python wheels bundle their own Qt and may lag behind.
