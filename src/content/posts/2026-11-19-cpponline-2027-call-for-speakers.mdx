The call for speakers for C++Online 2027 closes this Friday, 20 November 2026. The conference is online only and runs on 10 to 12 March 2027. The organisers asked their community partners to pass the reminder on, and this post does that.

## In short

- Proposals for talks close on Friday 20 November 2026. The organisers' message says "midnight UTC".
- The conference runs on 10 to 12 March 2027, from 12:00 to 22:00 UTC each day, online only.
- Talks are 30, 60 or 90 minutes, livestreamed and available on demand. Pre-recorded talks are accepted.
- The call for workshops and training courses is a separate call. The two sources I read give it different deadlines.

## Call for speakers

The details below are from the [call for speakers page](https://cpponline.uk/call-for-speakers/), which I read on 18 November 2026. The deadline there is 20 November 2026. The organisers' message to partners says the same, and words it as Friday at midnight UTC. I did not check whether that means the start or the end of the day. I would not leave a submission to the last hour.

A talk can be 30, 60 or 90 minutes long. It is livestreamed during the conference and stays available on demand afterwards. The page says pre-recorded talks are also accepted.

## Call for workshops and training courses

The [call for workshops](https://cpponline.uk/call-for-workshops/) covers half-day, full-day and multi-day online sessions. They run between April and June 2027, after the main conference. The page says there is an optional preview session during the conference.

The deadline needs care. The organisers' message to partners says the call for workshops also closes on Friday 20 November at midnight UTC. The call page I read on 18 November lists 1 December 2026 and says proposals are reviewed as they arrive. I cannot tell which is current. If you plan to propose a workshop, treat 20 November as the date and ask the organisers about 1 December.

## What I did not check

I did not read the submission forms, the selection criteria or any terms for speakers. I did not follow the graphics link in the organisers' message. The only pages I read are the two linked above.
