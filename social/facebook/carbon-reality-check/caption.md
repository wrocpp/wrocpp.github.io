# Carbon is still pre-0.1, with 1.0 somewhere after 2028

## Body
Carbon comes up whenever C++ succession is discussed, usually as though it were a choice a team could make. It is not one yet.

Carbon has not reached 0.1. Its own roadmap puts 0.1 at late 2026 at the earliest and 1.0 after 2028, and the repository still calls it an experiment that may not succeed. No stable specification, no ABI commitment. That honesty is the most useful thing about it.

Its premise is narrower than "a better C++": it targets codebases too large to rewrite and too active to freeze, accepting C++'s object model in exchange for interop without wrappers. Whether that works at scale is what the experiment is testing.

Meanwhile the realistic answers stay hardened C++ plus selective Rust.
